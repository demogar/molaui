# Data visualisation

<DecisionsBehind foundation="Foundations/Data visualisation" />

Mola UI ships the small charts a working screen needs, and the rules for drawing anything larger.
It does not ship a chart library, and it does not have a categorical palette. Every colour in the
system already means something, so a chart takes its colours from the same roles as the rest of
the screen.

## What ships

- **[Stat](?path=/docs/components-data-display-stat--overview)** with a `Sparkline`: an ink line with
  no fill and no axis, and the latest point drawn as a gold square with an ink keyline. It is
  decorative, because the figure and the change beside it already say what it shows.
- **[Progress and Meter](?path=/docs/components-feedback-progress--overview)**: how far a task has
  gone, and where a reading sits against its warning and danger thresholds.
- **[Token usage](?path=/docs/ai-token-usage--overview)**: a model's context budget, spent and
  remaining.

For anything else (a line chart of runs per day, a bar chart of cost per agent), bring your own
chart library and draw it by the rules below.

## Colour

<SeriesTable />

Each series is a graphic mark, so it owes 3:1 against every ground a chart sits on (WCAG 1.4.11).
The ratios above are computed from `tokens.css`. The contrast contract promises every one of them,
so a palette change that broke a series would fail the build.

- **Start in ink.** Most charts need one colour. Bars that carry their own labels need no colour to
  tell them apart, and a bar chart sorted by value reads better than a coloured one.
- **The comparison is muted and dashed.** Last week, the target, the baseline. Dashed, so it is
  still told apart in greyscale and by someone who cannot tell the colours apart.
- **Add a colour only when two lines must be told apart at a glance**, and stop at four. Añil,
  verde and the warning gold also mean information, success and warning. Name each line at its end
  so that its colour is never the only key, and give each line its own dash or marker as well.
  Past four lines, use a table or small multiples.
- **Rojo is never a series.** On a product screen it means something can be lost or has failed
  ([ADR 0008](?path=/docs/decisions-0008-the-rojo-register--overview)). Use it for a threshold that
  has been crossed, through the danger role (`--danger`), and say so in words.
- **Gold marks one point, not a line.** It measures under 3:1 on light cloth, so it is a square with
  an ink keyline, as the sparkline draws "now".
- **Gridlines are `--keyline`.** It is under 3:1 on purpose, so a gridline can never be read as data
  or as a control edge.

## Shape and type

- **No curves and no gradients.** Lines are mitred polylines, points are squares, and areas are flat
  fills. The system has no radius, and a chart is not an exception.
- **Figures are Archivo with tabular numerals** (the `tabular` utility), right-aligned in tables.
  Martian Mono is for machine literals, not for numbers
  ([ADR 0003](?path=/docs/decisions-0003-monospace-for-literals--overview)).
- **Label directly.** Put the series name at the end of its line and the value on the bar. A legend
  sends the eye away from the data and back.

## A worked example

<RunsChart />

This week in ink and last week dashed in muted ink. Today's point is a gold square with an ink
keyline, and each line is named at its end. Under the chart, the same numbers are in a table,
which is the chart's text alternative. A chart is an image to a screen reader. Give it a name that
says what it shows, and put the numbers somewhere a screen reader can read them.

## Checklist

1. One colour unless two series must be told apart, and never more than four.
2. No rojo unless something has failed or crossed a danger threshold, and then the words say so.
3. Each series named on the chart, with a dash or marker as well as its colour.
4. The numbers available as text: a table, or the figure beside the chart.
5. Checked in both themes and in greyscale.
