# Motion

<DecisionsBehind foundation="Foundations/Motion" />

Cloth settling under a hand: fast in, no float, **no overshoot anywhere**. One easing curve and
three durations, and motion is there to say what changed, never to decorate. A tool someone uses
for eight hours a day cannot make them wait for its animations.

## Durations and easing

The durations are tokens in `tokens.css`; the easing is `--ease-cut` in `theme.css`, used as
`ease-cut`. The "used by" column is read from the components, not intended use.

| Token | Value | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--motion-cut` | 120ms | Hover and press on every control: a button's colour and band, a field's edge, a checkbox, a tab's colour. Also every **exit**. |
| `--motion-base` | 220ms | Things that enter or move: menus, popovers, dialogs, drawers and the mobile sidebar coming in, a switch thumb, the tabs indicator, an accordion panel, a disclosure marker turning, a progress bar filling. |
| `--motion-slow` | 420ms | One place today: a toast sliding as the stack reflows. |
| `ease-cut` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` | Everything that eases. Fast out of the start, settles without passing its mark. |

**Out is faster than in.** Overlays enter at `--motion-base` and leave at `--motion-cut`. Someone
who opened a menu wants to see where it came from; someone who closed it has already moved on.

A menu, popover or dialog that enters fades in and grows from 98% of its size, from the origin
Base UI computes, so a menu grows out of its trigger rather than out of its own centre. Only a drawer
travels, because it belongs to an edge of the screen; nothing bounces or overshoots.

## Layout snaps

Motion moves with `opacity`, `translate` and `scale`, which the compositor draws without laying
the page out again. Nothing transitions `width`, `height`, the insets, margin or padding, and
nothing transitions `all`, which reaches them. Each frame of a layout transition lays out
everything that depends on it, and in this system that is usually a data table with measured
columns and container queries.

So the app shell's sidebar **snaps** between its full width and the rail: the work area changes
width once, not on every frame of a 220ms ease. It used to ease, on the grounds that the content
takes the room the rail gives up; the reflow cost more than the motion said. A collapse is a change
of layout, not a state to watch.

Two layout transitions are kept, each with its reason: an accordion or collapsible panel opening
to the height Base UI measures, because what is below it has to move and scaling would squash the
text; and the toast stack, which is fixed and out of the page's flow. `motion.test.ts` fails any
other in the source, and the browser audit fails any other it finds running.

## Motion that carries meaning

A few animations are not transitions but states. Each one is a named keyframe in `theme.css`.

- **`mola-shuttle`**, the working relleno. Slits sliding past say "this is being cut": an agent
  step running, a file uploading, a progress bar with no total. The system has no spinner.
- **`mola-pulse`**, a live status dot, at 1.6s. Used on a badge with `pulse` and a running toast.
- **`mola-caret`**, the streaming caret. Stepped, not faded: a block of ink, on or off, at 1.05s.
- **`mola-shimmer`**, a sweep of raised cloth across a skeleton while a slot is still empty.
- **`mola-in`**, a 4px settle and fade on `--motion-base`, offered as `animate-mola-in` for content
  of your own that appears in place. No component uses it yet; overlays get the same settle from
  their transitions.

## Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, one rule in `base.css` collapses every animation and
transition in the document to a single frame (`0.01ms`, one iteration). Nothing is switched off
component by component, so a new component is covered the day it is written.

The states are designed to survive that, because each still says something true when it stops:

- **The working relleno is stilled, not hidden.** A static band of slits still reads as
  "in progress".
- **The caret stops blinking and stays.** The text is still arriving.
- **A pulsing dot stops pulsing; the dot stays**, and so does its word.
- **The skeleton's sweep is removed outright** (`motion-reduce:after:hidden`), not slowed. The
  placeholder is left as a still, textured slot, which still says something is coming.
- **Menus, dialogs and accordions appear without travelling.** They still open and close; they
  snap instead of settle. An accordion's height comes from Base UI's measurement, so snapping
  lands on the right size.
- **Streamed text still streams.** Reduced motion stops decoration, not information: the words
  arrive as fast as the model sends them.

Check it in Chromium with DevTools › Rendering › *Emulate CSS media feature
prefers-reduced-motion*, or in macOS under Accessibility › Display › Reduce motion.
