# Layers and elevation

<DecisionsBehind foundation="Foundations/Layers and elevation" />

Most design systems say how far something is from the page with a shadow: a card casts a small
one, a menu a larger one, a dialog the largest. Mola UI says it with **layers of cloth and the
edges cut through them**, the method a mola is made with. A surface is a layer; its edge is a cut,
drawn in ink; and when something is chosen or pointed at, the cut opens to show the layer beneath.
There is exactly one blur in the system, and only for what genuinely floats.

This page borrows the method, not the motifs. Nothing on it, or anywhere in the interface, copies a
Guna design, symbol or pattern.

## The stack

From the bottom up, these are every depth an interface built with Mola UI has.

**Raised is a colour and an edge, not a shadow.** `--cloth-pale` is lighter than the page in both
themes, so "raised" means the same thing in light and dark, and the ink keyline says where the
layer ends. A card, an input, a menu and a dialog are all raised cloth; what tells them apart is
whether they float.

**One step down** is `--cloth-shade`: a region pressed below the page, such as a table's header
row or a well that holds read-only content. It is the same keyline with a darker cloth, never an
inner shadow.

## The edge: a cut, not a border

Every edge a person can touch is `shadow-cut`: 1.5px of ink drawn as a `box-shadow`. A border
would take up layout space, so the hover that grows an edge into a band would push every
neighbour along. A shadow paints outside the box and moves nothing. `border-keyline` exists, but
only for decorative dividers between things nobody clicks, and it measures under 3:1 on purpose so
nobody mistakes it for a control.

## The reveal: cut-band and band-\*

When a control is hovered or chosen, its cut opens: ink, then a band of colour, then ink again.
The band is concentric, never offset to one side, because the layers of a mola are stacked and
the reveal goes all the way round. `band-*` chooses the layer underneath.

Gold is the default reveal: it is the band a hover shows on a button, a field, a checkbox or a
chip. Red is revealed only on something already about danger, because a field that flashes red
under the pointer reads, for a moment, as an error.

## Floating: the one blur

Menus, popovers, selects, the command palette, dialogs, drawers and toasts float above the page,
and they are the only things with a blur. They wear `shadow-floating`, which is the keyline **and**
`--elevation-raised` together, from one shared surface (`floatingSurface` in the popover). Tailwind
keeps one `box-shadow` per element, so the two have to be declared as one utility; written as
`shadow-cut shadow-raised`, one of them silently wins.

The keyline is never optional on a floating surface, and the dark theme is the reason. A blur is a
darker patch of the ground under the surface, and on a near-black ground there is almost nothing
darker to paint. In the light theme the blur does part of the work; in the dark one it all but
disappears, and the keyline is what still says "this is above the page". Switch the toolbar's
theme on an overlay story, such as the [menu of row actions](?path=/story/components-overlays-menu--row-actions),
to see it.

A dialog also dims what is behind it, toward the page's own colour (`--backdrop`) rather than
toward grey.

## Seams and fillers: diente and relleno

Two more devices come from the same method, and neither is depth.

- **Diente**, the sawtooth seam, marks the edge where the page gives way to a filled panel. It is
  used rarely, on major seams only (the `Divider` with `variant="diente"`). In a mola this edge is
  the slow, expensive cut, so there are only a few.
- **Relleno**, the filler slits, fills space that has nothing in it yet: a divider in place of a
  hairline, an empty state's texture, and, moving, the **working** state for work whose size is
  unknown. See [The cut](?path=/docs/foundations-the-cut--overview) for each one drawn.

## Which layer, when

| Surface | Layer | Utilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The page | Ground | `bg-cloth` |
| Card, input, panel | Raised | `bg-cloth-pale shadow-cut` |
| Table header, well, disabled field | One step down | `bg-cloth-shade` |
| Hovered or selected control | Revealed | `cut-band band-oro` (or `band-anil`, `band-rojo`) |
| Menu, popover, select, dialog, drawer, toast | Floating | `bg-cloth-pale shadow-floating` |
| A whole region that belongs to a status | Wash | `bg-anil-soft`, `bg-verde-soft`… (see Color) |
| The edge between the page and a filled panel | Seam | `diente` |

Two things never appear: a radius, and a shadow on something that does not float. A card with a
soft drop shadow is a different system's costume.

## In forced colours

Windows High Contrast removes every `box-shadow`, so every keyline, band and blur goes at once.
`shadow-cut`, `shadow-floating`, `cut` and `cut-band` each draw a 1px outline in the user's text
colour in that mode, so the layers keep their edges. The bands and the blur are decoration and go
with the mode, which is the point of it. See
[Accessibility](?path=/docs/foundations-accessibility--overview) for the rest.
