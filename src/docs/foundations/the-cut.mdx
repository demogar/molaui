# The cut

The devices that make this a mola rather than a palette swap. Each is a Tailwind v4 `@utility`
in `src/styles/cloth.css`, so it composes with everything else.

## Keyline and revealed band

`shadow-cut` is the blade edge. `cut-band` adds the revealed layer and a second edge —
concentric, never offset: a mola's layers are stacked, so the reveal goes all the way around.
`band-*` chooses the colour beneath, per element.

Edges are `box-shadow`, not `border`, because the signature hover *grows* the edge by several
pixels. A border would reflow the row; a shadow does not move a single neighbour.

## Hover

Two gestures, chosen by whether the control has an edge:

- **A cut shape grows its band.** Buttons, fields, checkboxes, chips and cards reveal the layer
  beneath on hover — gold by default, never red.
- **An edgeless control washes.** Ghost buttons, rows, menu items and disclosure triggers take
  `--ink-soft` behind them, and secondary text steps up to ink.

Hover is never rojo, except on the danger button that is already red. Red means something can be
lost or has failed; a heading or a field that flashes red as the pointer crosses it reads, for a
moment, as an error. The editorial system turned links and accordion titles red on hover, and the
product system dropped it for that reason.

## Relleno — the filler slit

In a finished mola the negative space is packed with narrow slits cut to a lower layer. Here it is
the divider (in place of a hairline), the empty-state texture, and — moving — the **working**
state: work is happening and its size is unknown.

The layer colour is the *ground* and ink is the *slit*. Inverted — thick colour bars on black — the
strip reads as hazard tape. That was the first build of it.

## Diente — the sawtooth seam

Where a top layer is cut back. Used only on major seams, where the page ground gives way to a
filled panel; in a real mola this edge is the slow, expensive part, and there are only a few.

## Display, cut

The mola applied to letterforms: fill, then the revealed band, then the ink edge. **Only on a
filled panel**, one per page. On light cloth a bold sans in a gold-and-black outline stops reading
as appliqué and starts reading as a comic-book sticker.

    <span className="display-cut band-oro text-ink">Cayuco</span>
  </p>
</Specimen>

## Focus

A third band: two-tone, so it survives on any ground — a cloth gap, then ink. Square, because the
radius is zero. In Windows High Contrast, where shadows are removed, a system-coloured outline
takes over.

</Specimen>
